Mander × Rewind NYC · Redesign brief

Rewind is already premium in person. The site should be too.

Rewind has the room, the story, the team and the booking infrastructure. The concept keeps all of it — the dark palette, the wordmark, the boombox door, one era per room, SevenRooms, the inquiry form — and fixes how it's presented.

About the demo: it's a visual redesign concept built with Rewind's own photos, video, fonts and logo animations. The forms are for show and don't send anything. SEO isn't built into the demo; it's described at the end of this brief.

What's holding the current site back

Measured on rewindnyc.com in September 2026. None of this is visible from the front, but it costs speed, search rankings and leads.

Speed

  • The Karaoke page is 3 MB of HTML. 2.7 MB of that is photos embedded directly in the page code as base64. Browsers can't cache them or load them lazily, so the whole page is slow to appear. The Lounge page is 1.75 MB and Private Events 1.1 MB, for the same reason.
  • The videos are 540p. That's soft on laptops and desktop monitors, and they're cropped into half-screen panels, which makes them softer still.

Search

  • No page has an H1 heading. It's the main signal search engines use for what a page is about.
  • Every page shares the same title: "REWIND NYC — Karaoke & Lounge · Long Island City." Karaoke, Lounge and Private Events can't rank for different searches.
  • Images have empty alt text: all 20 on the Karaoke page, 36 of 54 on the Lounge page.
  • The menu is a Canva link, and rewindnyc.com/menu returns a 404.

Conversion

  • "Reservations" opens an unstyled SevenRooms list, and the private-event link is small text at the bottom.
  • "Email Us" is a mailto link, which does nothing on phones or work laptops without a mail app.
  • The Karaoke page has no route to book or inquire from the room photos themselves.

Why a visual overhaul, not a patch

Fixing these means rebuilding how pages are put together: real image files, proper headings, per-page titles and a real menu page. Doing that inside the current layout would preserve the thing that makes the site feel generic. Rebuilding lets us fix the plumbing and bring the design up to the level of the venue in one pass.

What changed in the concept

Eight things we found on the live site, and how the concept handles each.

01

Reservations

"Reservations" opens an unstyled SevenRooms list. The private-event link is small text at the bottom.

A branded three-step drawer: pick the experience, choose date and party size, then continue to SevenRooms with it prefilled. "Planning something bigger?" is on every step.

02

"Email Us"

A mailto link. It does nothing on phones and work laptops without a mail app, and leaves no structured lead.

A short on-page contact form with a topic picker. The address stays visible as plain text for anyone who prefers email.

03

Room grid

Six photos with mismatched color and crops, labels stamped on the image, and copy that promises eight rooms.

One crop system and grade, a name and mood line per room, capacity and a starting rate on every card.

04

Rates

A dense table that reads like a policy PDF.

Three cards, one per room size, with the late-night weekend minimum called out. Add-ons sit underneath with a Book button.

05

Speed and motion

No loading state and no motion. Hero videos are 540p, which is soft on any modern screen.

A short loader built on Rewind's own logo animation, and one orchestrated hero entrance. Rewind's own footage is AI-upscaled to 1080p, and video plays only when on screen, which keeps the page light.

06

Private events and buyouts

The highest-value lead is only reachable by digging into the reservation popup.

It's on the first screen, and it has its own cinematic section with the three spaces (30 / 70 / 100 guests), the key terms, and the existing inquiry form restyled with validation and a clear confirmation.

07

Menu

"Menu" opens a Canva document outside the site, and rewindnyc.com/menu returns a 404. The dish photos on the Lounge page have no names.

An on-page Food & Drink section. Guests pick one of the Five Elements to see its cocktail, and swipe through named dishes. It links to the full menu.

08

Layout

Every section is a centered heading over a narrow column of text, repeated down the page.

Editorial grids that alternate full-bleed images with quieter type sections, set in Rewind's own Josefin Sans with a serif italic for accents.

What we'd do in the full build

The concept is one page. The engagement rolls the system across the site.

Design & build

  • Home, Lounge, Karaoke, Private Events, Our Story, Menu, Events, FAQ and Press on one design system
  • Room pages with galleries and "book this room"
  • Events calendar for Between Sips and ticketed nights
  • Mobile-first, with an accessibility pass (contrast, keyboard, screen reader)

Media & performance

  • A photo and video pass, or upscaling and regrading of the existing library
  • Responsive images, lazy loading, short muted loops
  • SevenRooms widget themed to match the site
  • Inquiry form wired to the events inbox or CRM, with lead tracking

SEO recommendations

Suggestions only. None of this is built into the demo; it's what we'd set up in the full build.

  • private karaoke NYC
  • private karaoke Long Island City
  • corporate event space Long Island City
  • private event venue NYC
  • karaoke party NYC
  • karaoke room Queens

On-page

  • A dedicated, indexable /private-events page with an H1 aimed at corporate buyouts in Long Island City
  • One H1 per page (there are none today), with a clean H2 structure under it
  • Descriptive alt text on every photo, starting with the rooms and dishes
  • A unique title and meta description per page (today all pages share one)
  • Internal links between Karaoke, Private Events and the menu
  • Publish the menu as a real page instead of a Canva link. Search engines can't read it today, and dish names like "wagyu don" and "egg sando" are things people search for near Long Island City.

Structured data & local

  • Extend the existing BarOrPub schema with event capacity, price range and room types
  • FAQPage schema on the FAQ (the answers already exist)
  • Event schema for Between Sips and ticketed nights
  • Align the Google Business Profile: categories, event-space photos, Q&A
  • Listings on Peerspace and Tagvenue, where corporate planners search

Content

Short, useful pages that match how planners search, for example "Corporate karaoke party in NYC: what to plan," "Team events in Long Island City" and "Birthday karaoke in Queens." Each links to the inquiry form. The existing press coverage (NBC New York, Eater NY, World Journal) also works as trust content and for backlinks.
