Mander × Rewind NYC · Redesign brief

Rewind is premium in person. Online, it doesn't look it yet.

Rewind has a press-covered room, a bar program from Asia's 50 Best alumni, a Michelin-trained chef and eight private karaoke rooms. The website is how most guests and event planners meet all of that first, and right now it's slow, hard to find on Google, and makes the most valuable bookings hard to start.

Why it's worth a redesign

The site is the first room guests walk into

Diners and planners decide from their phone before they ever find the unmarked staircase. Right now the page loads slowly and flashes a fallback font before the brand font arrives, and it doesn't feel like the venue Eater and NBC wrote about.

Private events are the biggest checks, and the hardest to start

Corporate planners shortlist venues online, often after hours. Today the inquiry path is a small link at the bottom of a reservations popup. A buyout lead deserves a front-door entrance.

Google can't tell the pages apart

With no H1s, one shared title and unlabeled images, Rewind is invisible for "private karaoke Long Island City" and "corporate event space LIC." Those are the searches that bring in events.

A refresh, not a rebrand

The identity is strong: the wordmark, Josefin Sans, the boombox door, the Five Elements, the koi and bird animations. We keep all of it and fix the layout, speed and structure around it, so the brand finally reads online the way it does in the room.

What we audited and fixed in the demo

01

Speed

Photos are embedded as base64 in the page code (3 MB Karaoke page). Fonts sit behind seven chained stylesheet imports and ship as uncompressed TTF/OTF from a folder named "local-development".

Real, compressed image files that load as you scroll. Video plays only when on screen. A short loader built on Rewind's own logo animation.

02

Video

Hero clips are 540p, which is soft on any laptop.

Rewind's own footage, AI-upscaled to 1080p (1440p for large screens), graded to match.

03

Reservations

An unstyled button list, then straight to SevenRooms.

A branded three-step drawer, with a Book button on every room card and a Reserve bar that stays on screen on phones.

04

Private events

A small link at the bottom of the reservations popup.

On the first screen, in every reservation step, and in its own section with the three spaces (30 / 70 / 100 guests) and the inquiry form.

05

"Email Us"

A mailto link, which does nothing on phones or work laptops without a mail app.

An on-page contact form with a topic picker.

06

Menu

A Canva link; rewindnyc.com/menu is a 404; dish photos have no names.

An on-page Food & Drink section: pick one of the Five Elements to see its cocktail, and swipe through named dishes.

07

Rooms and rates

Unnamed room photos and a dense rate table.

Named rooms with mood, capacity and price; rate cards per size.

08

Layout and type

Every section is a centered heading over a narrow column, repeated down the page.

Editorial grids that alternate full-bleed photography with quieter type, set in Rewind's own Josefin Sans with a serif italic for accents.

In the final build: reserving and inquiring, done properly

Reserve

  • SevenRooms embedded and styled to match the site, so guests never feel they've left
  • Room-level Book buttons that open the right room with date and party size carried over
  • A Reserve bar that stays on screen on phones, where most bookings start
  • Clear policy up front (Tuesdays closed, late-night minimums, deposit) so fewer bookings bounce

Private-event inquiry form

  • Wired to Rewind's events inbox or CRM, with an instant branded auto-reply
  • An optional budget field and "how did you hear about us" so the team can prioritize
  • An events PDF (spaces, packages, floor plans) sent with the auto-reply
  • Spam protection, plus tracking in the Google Analytics tag Rewind already has, so every lead is counted

Weeks 1–2

Fix the foundations

  • Real image files, compressed and lazy-loaded
  • One H1 per page; a unique title and description per page
  • Alt text on every photo
  • Faster font loading (WOFF2, no chained imports)
  • Structured data: BarOrPub with capacity, FAQPage, Event
Weeks 2–4

Pages people search for

  • A dedicated Private Events page aimed at corporate buyouts in Long Island City
  • A real HTML menu (dish names like "wagyu don" and "egg sando" are searched)
  • A page per karaoke room
  • An FAQ built from the questions planners actually ask
Ongoing

Local presence

  • Google Business Profile: categories, event-space photos, Q&A
  • Listings on Peerspace and Tagvenue, where corporate planners search
  • Links back from existing press (Eater, NBC, World Journal)
  • Short guides, such as "planning a corporate karaoke night in NYC," that link to the inquiry form

Other suggestions

Reach

  • A Chinese-language page. Rewind is already on RedNote and in World Journal; a page in Chinese serves that audience directly.
  • An events calendar for Between Sips and special nights, with tickets or RSVPs.
  • Corporate packages (e.g. "team night for 20," "full buyout") with starting prices, so planners can budget before they write.

Revenue & operations

  • Gift cards for karaoke hours and lounge tabs.
  • A monthly report on leads, bookings and search traffic, so the site's value is measurable.
  • An accessibility pass (contrast, keyboard, screen readers), which also helps SEO.
